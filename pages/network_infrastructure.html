The goal

A good network is one you never have to talk about.

Nobody wants to think about their connection. They want the call not to drop, the file to upload, and the room at the back of the building to have signal.

That is the whole brief. Everything below exists to make the network boring — and to keep it that way once we have gone.

How an install runs

Survey first. Cable once.

Most bad networks were not installed badly — they were installed before anyone had walked the building. So that is where we start.

  1. 01

    Survey

    We walk the site, find where the signal actually dies, and note the walls, distances and dead corners that decide everything after this.

  2. 02

    Design

    A layout: where the line lands, where the access points go, how the network is divided, and what it costs — written down before anything is ordered.

  3. 03

    Install

    Cabling run properly and labelled, equipment mounted and configured, and the whole thing tested in the rooms people will actually use it in.

  4. 04

    Monitor

    Quiet checks on the connection afterwards, so the usual outcome is us telling you something went wrong rather than the other way round.
